Upcoming Tech Conferences: What Founders Should Watch Next

Tech Week is a city-based series of startup and technology events spread over several days, where founders, investors, and builders meet through talks, demos, and community gatherings. Instead of one big venue, the activity is spread across many small events, which makes it a practical way for founders to meet useful people in a short time.

15 Points for Founders

  1. Set one clear goal first, such as customers, investors, hires, or partners.
  2. Choose events by audience quality, not crowd size.
  3. Learn the seasonal pattern. Many events return in similar months each year, so you can plan budgets early.
  4. Watch for official date announcements and confirm them before you book anything.
  5. Book travel and lodging early once dates are confirmed, because prices rise near popular weeks.
  6. Prepare a ten second pitch and a one minute version.
  7. Build a list of 20 people you want to meet and research each one.
  8. Favor small side events. At Tech Week, smaller gatherings often lead to deeper conversations than main stages.
  9. Notice which topics repeat across talks, because repeated themes show where money and attention are moving.
  10. Watch who is hiring and who is raising, since both reveal market momentum.
  11. Keep part of your schedule open for unplanned introductions.
  12. Bring a simple demo or one page summary that works well on a phone.
  13. Write notes within an hour of each meeting while the details are fresh.
  14. Follow up within 48 hours with a short message and one clear next step.
  15. Track cost against results, then decide whether to return next year.

Plan the Whole Cycle

Treat every Tech Week as a three part process: prepare, attend, and follow up. Founders who only do the middle part usually leave with a stack of contacts and little else. Whether you join a Tech Week in your home city or travel for one, the same discipline applies.

Start planning a few months ahead, keep a simple record of every conversation, and review your results afterward. A good Tech Week outcome is a handful of real relationships, not a full inbox.
